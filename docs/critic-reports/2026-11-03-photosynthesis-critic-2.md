## MED
1. The student sentence ("using the smallest whole-number coefficients") does not rule out the equation the key refuses, and the key's reason is false. 6, 12, 1, 6, 6 have no common factor, so 6CO2 + 12H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O already IS in the smallest whole numbers; the real reason it is not the answer is that water appears on both sides (not the net equation). Key says "Not accepted: coefficients that are not the smallest whole numbers, such as ..."; FAQ calls them "larger coefficients"; the builder's test asserts the form is balanced and that "smallest" rules it out (false). A student with the Campbell form is marked wrong on a rebuttable reason. Fix: student "Write the net balanced equation (no substance on both sides)..."; key and FAQ "Not accepted: water on both sides...".
2. "Read to the nearest fine gridline" can't be judged by eye in many states and the key accepts only one gridline (range is exactly half a fine spacing either side). Repro: CO2, Plateau 7.5, half-saturation empty (120), P auto (400 ppm), student copy: exact rate 5.769, fine gridlines 5.5 and 6.0 only 11.5 px apart, P's guide 5.3 px below 6.0 and 6.2 px above 5.5, dot covers ~10 of 11.5 px, key accepts 5.52-6.02 so a student writing 5.5 is marked wrong. 17.6% of 29,504 random typed states have the exact rate within 0.1 of a fine spacing of the midpoint; no note warns. Opening states are fine (0.333, 0.385, 0.778 of a spacing above the lower line at plateau 10). Fix: accept and print both neighbouring gridlines when within ~0.25 spacing of the midpoint, or accept +/-1 fine spacing, or add an editor note.
3. In the default Colour style the fine gridlines the student must read are almost invisible: FINE_COLOUR #e9ecf2 at 0.7 px is 1.18:1 on white (labelled #d3d9e3 about 1.45:1); the student sheet says to read to the nearest fine gridline; thresholded at 66% on a B/W laser none survive at 300 or 150 dpi (Colour 0/6 labelled, 0/18 fine); nothing tells the teacher the task needs Print. Fix: fine to >= about #b8c0cc, labelled to ~#9aa4b2, or use the Print greys in student copies, or a note.
