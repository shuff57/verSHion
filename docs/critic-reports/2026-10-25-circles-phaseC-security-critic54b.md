## Blockers (new class)

### N1. Withheld versions are pinned while their flag is open, with no bound: one editor can clog a circle's storage so that nobody but an administrator can clear it (round 46 V7 fix)
- `server/worker/src/pool/circlesend.ts:134,140` (`PRUNABLE_SQL` and `PINNED_EVIDENCE_SQL`), `:938` (the commit's prune), `circleversions.ts` trim (the `tf.kind IN ${SEND_FLAG_KINDS}` clause, ~:191), and `circles.ts` `OPEN_FLAG_BANK` / `pendingReview` (purge blocked while a flag is open).
- Exploit (`c54/pin.test.ts`): a takedown exists for a text T. An editor X of a circle sends a version containing T plus a padding question, then 60 more such versions (each withheld, each with its own `blocklist` flag). Result: `circle_versions` has 1 current + 60 `previous` rows all with bodies (the keep-20 rule is gone), 61 open flags; the owner's `POST .../trim {keep:1}` answers `200 {"pruned":0, "pinned_versions":[]}` (and says nothing about why); the owner's `DELETE` of the bank answers 200 but the 409 `pending_review` purge never happens and the bytes stay counted (`circle_bytes`). Nothing the owner or the author does frees them; only an administrator resolving 61 flags one by one does.
- Size: `MAX_PAYLOAD` is 4 MB (`publish.ts:108`), `circle_commits` is 100 a day, `circle_bytes` is 200 MB: one editor account fills a circle's whole quota in under a day (about 50 sends of 4 MB), after which every member's sends are refused with the storage-full message, and the owner has no remedy. The same works with confirmed student data in place of copyright (round 38 pin). Before round 46 these versions were pruned like any other. Bonus: the same account gets 100 admin flags a day (alert mails are budgeted, the flag rows are not). The held-publish counter (`HELD_PUBLISHES_PER_DAY` 20) only raises an admin alert; it refuses nothing.
- Needs: any editor of a circle (a team's usual member) and one active takedown whose text the attacker has (a publicly known text before takedown). Severity: medium-high (data/storage denial, owner-uncleanable, admin-queue flooding).
- Fix: bound the pin (e.g. at most 3 pinned versions per bank, 10 per circle, oldest evidence first prunes anyway; or pin only the newest per sender), and refuse a sender's next send into a bank while it already has N open flags from that sender; have trim list `pinned_versions` (it is empty here although 60 were kept).

### N2. `/admin/dmca/unblock {release_circle_versions: true}` releases a version whose text is now confirmed student data
- `server/worker/src/pool/circleadmin.ts:51-88` (`circleUnblock`): the only guard is "no open `privacy_text_match` flag on this version". A version withheld for copyright carries no such flag even when its text matches a student-data block that was confirmed AFTER the send (circles are never rescanned).
- Repro (`c54/rel.test.ts`): takedown of item1 (text T), an editor sends T (withheld, `blocklist` flag), the admin privacy-purges a second public item with T (`privacy_blocks` gains the hash), then unblock with `release_circle_versions: true` answers `{"circle_flags_lifted":1,"circle_versions_released":1}`; the owner now reads the version in full (the text contains the "student"), and `circle_qkeys` joined to `privacy_blocks` still matches the bank. The release is a bulk side effect of a route about copyright; the admin is not shown the match.
- Severity: medium (admin-driven but silent, and it unhides confirmed student data). Fix: in `circleUnblock` also require no `privacy_blocks` row matching the bank's `circle_qkeys` (the `PRIVACY_MATCH_SQL` of the same file) before releasing, or only mark `block_lifted` and leave the release to the explicit audited `circle-release`.

## Variants and limits

- V1. Per-domain hourly cap is a mail DoS with no reserved share (`circlemail.ts:150`, cap 30/hour; the reserved fifth only applies to the site cap at `:152`). `m1.test.ts` M2: 3 approved accounts at 10 mails each to random addresses at `gmail.com` made the next legitimate gmail invite `503 busy` ("Too many invitations are being sent right now"), another domain still worked, the next hour recovered. For a deployment where most teachers share one domain (a district) three accounts block everyone's invitations for the domain for an hour, twice a day each (20/day). The same accounts also cause real mail to arbitrary addresses (bounces against the sender's reputation). Fix: keep a share of the domain budget for inviters under `EMAIL_SMALL_INVITER`, or cap one inviter at a fraction of a domain.
- V2. Provider-side aliases other than Gmail's are only bounded by the domain cap: `me.com`/`mac.com`/`icloud.com`, `pm.me`/`proton.me`, Fastmail subdomain addressing, catch-all or Workspace domains with dot-insensitive names. One account can send 20 a day to one such mailbox, N accounts 30 an hour.
- V3. `+x@host` (empty local part after the tag strip) all fold to `@host` (shared cap key, harmless: never deliverable); `emailCapKey` also treats `+` as a tag on every domain (a real `a+b@corp` mailbox is capped together with `a@corp`; fails closed).
- V4. Information in the answers (53b's "account-exists oracle" checked: no lookup by address, same statements, no awaited difference, `not_deliverable` depends only on the string). What is new: `address_limit` depends on how many mails ANY inviter sent that mailbox today, and it is answered before anything is charged (no daily slot, no hourly slot). An approved account can ask "has this mailbox had 3 invitations today" at the cost of a general request, and can learn the count 0-2 by inviting until the answer flips (it mails the target). The same for `busy` on a domain (30 an hour). It discloses only that colleagues are being invited somewhere, never who or where. Low.
- V5. Masked label `j***@gmail.com` (`circlemail.ts` `maskEmail`): shown only to the circle's owner (`listInvites` `requireRole(owner)`), and in the inviter's own export. For a one-character local part it is the whole local part; for a district domain the initial + domain narrows a person a lot; it is plain text in `circle_invites` until 30 days after expiry (about 37 days), kept on used and revoked invites, nulled by erasure of the inviter (`circleErasureStatements`/`circleResweepStatements`, both updated) but NOT by the invitee's erasure (no link to an account exists). A new owner after a transfer sees the old owner's labels. The cron clears 500 labels a run (`LIMIT 500`), so a backlog beyond 500 expiries a day lives longer.
- V6. Flag `actions` is truncated to 50 takedowns (`circlesend.ts` privacyScan `.slice(0, 50)`), so an unblock of those 50 can release a version a 51st active block also held. Needs more than 50 takedowns matching one version; unreal today.
- V7. `giveDaily` uses the current day, so a give-back after midnight lowers the NEW day's count (clamped at 0): at most one free slot, no negative counters. A batch that commits but whose response is lost to the Worker (thrown after commit) is given back though the invite exists. Low.
- V8. PATCH hide_key CPU: head + older versions inside 4 MB, `cpu.test.ts`: 4.7 s wall for a 1,000-question bank with 10 versions (6.5 MB), of which most is wasted on older versions that need label decisions (the strip fails with no decisions, so they stay unrecorded). Every PATCH is charged (`circle_hide_changes` 20 a day) including 422 refusals; a no-op is free. For a typical bank with labels, turning hide on makes ALL older versions 409 to viewers (fail-closed; a product limit).
- V9. Rollback commit does not compare `hide_key` (`circlesend.ts:1296` `hideKey: null`; sends do, `:787`): a PATCH that turns hiding on between a rollback's check and its commit lets the rollback commit with its target's recorded decisions without the viewers' form having been scanned in this run. Narrow race; the form was scanned when those decisions were recorded, and a null record is refused at read.
- V10. Released-version audit: the `pool.dmca_unblock` line does not record `release_circle_versions`; the per-version `circle.admin_version_release` lines do (written in the same batch as the release).
