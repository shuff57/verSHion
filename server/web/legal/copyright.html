Copyright Policy

verSHion shared question pool. Last updated: 2026-10-22 (draft).

In short: if you think something in the pool copies your work, send us a notice with the details below. We disable the item, tell its author, and the author can answer with a counter-notice. We respond to notices that follow this policy. Accounts with repeated upheld infringement are reviewed and may be suspended.

1. Scope

This policy is about copyright. The pool is invite-only and only approved teachers can publish, but teachers can still publish something they had no right to share. The Terms require every publisher to confirm that the work is their own, or that they have the right to share it, and that it is not copied from a published source (textbooks, publisher test banks, exam boards, paid worksheets) without permission. Student personal data is a different issue; see the Privacy Policy.

We respond to notices that follow this policy, and we handle them in good faith. We make no claim that doing so gives us any particular legal protection. [CONFIRM: with counsel before launch]

3. What a notice must include

Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3), a notice of claimed infringement must be in writing and include:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of a person authorised to act for the owner of the exclusive right that is allegedly infringed.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed (or a representative list, if one notice covers several works).
  3. Identification of the material claimed to be infringing, and information reasonably sufficient for us to find it, such as the link to the item or its title and author name in the pool.
  4. Your contact information: address, phone number and email.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorised to act for the owner of the exclusive right allegedly infringed.

We cannot see items that are private or unlisted, so please be as specific as you can about where we can find the material. If a notice is missing elements, we record it as incomplete and contact you for the rest; we may not act until we have it.

4. What happens next

  1. Review. We check that the notice is complete. We record it, with each required element.
  2. The item is disabled. We take down the material identified. It disappears from the pool, search and the author’s public listings. The content is moved to a restricted quarantine where only we can read it. We may also hold later uploads of the same questions or images for review, and hold copies made from the item. Questions or images that three or more other authors use independently are normally not blocked.
  3. The author is told. We email the author and show the item as “taken down after a copyright notice” with a counter-notice form. The author’s own copy on their device is not affected. People who imported the item keep their copy but are told the source was removed, and why.
  4. The author has 30 days from being notified to file a counter-notice. If they do not, the takedown stands, the item is purged, and it counts as an upheld strike (see below). If the author’s account has been deleted, nobody can file a counter-notice, and the takedown stands after 30 days.
  5. If a complete counter-notice arrives, we send it to you straight away and tell you the material will be restored in 10 business days. We restore it no sooner than 10 and no later than 14 business days after we receive the counter-notice (weekends and US federal holidays are not counted), unless you tell us you have filed a court action against the author to restrain the infringement. If we hear that, we keep the material disabled until the matter is resolved. Please send us the court-action notice at the address above.
  6. Put-back. When restored, the item returns as it was, the strike is cancelled, and any blocks and holds the takedown caused are lifted.

[CONFIRM: the app notifies the complainant; these days are computed in UTC; the 30-day window is 30 calendar days]

5. Counter-notices

If you are the author of an item that was taken down and believe that was a mistake or a misidentification, use the counter-notice form in the app (or write to the agent above). Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3), a counter-notice must include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed or disabled, and where it appeared before it was removed.
  3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, address and phone number, a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district where your address is (or, if outside the United States, any judicial district where we may be found), and a statement that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the notice or their agent.

Incomplete counter-notices are not accepted by the form. We send a complete one to the person who sent the notice, including the contact details you give. Please know that by doing so you are agreeing to that.

6. Repeat infringers

We keep a repeat-infringer policy. Each takedown that is not reversed (no counter-notice by the end of the 30 days, or a court decision for the complainant) is an upheld strike against the author. A takedown that is withdrawn or put back is not a strike. When an account has 3 upheld strikes within a rolling 12 months, it is flagged for administrator review. We review the account and may suspend or close it. Suspending an account hides all its items.

This policy is published here and in the Terms. We may also suspend or end an account sooner for serious or deliberate infringement.

7. False claims

A notice or counter-notice is a legal statement. Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), a person who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages, including costs and attorneys’ fees. Please do not send a notice unless you have considered whether the use may be authorised, for example by a licence or a fair use. We may also take action against accounts that file abusive or knowingly false notices.

8. A note on licences

Items in the pool are shared under licences the author chose, including Creative Commons licences. Copying an item under the terms of its licence, with credit, is not infringement. If you want a licensed item removed because its author had no right to license it, use the notice process above.
